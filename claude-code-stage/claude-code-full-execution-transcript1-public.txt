Short answer: this dataset can support a small exploratory, hypothesis-generating look at pretreatment tumour expression versus ICB response. It cannot support calling anything a pretreatment predictive biomarker. There are at most 8–10 pretreatment patients, response is mixed up with batch, the group oftreated patients is a mix of regimens, nothing in the cohort can test whether an effect is specific to treatment, and there is no independent validation. I stopped before any gene-level testing, so no gene-level results are reported here.

1. File audit (actual outputs)

Count file (GSE168204_MGH_counts.csv.gz, sha256 4e3ec53c…): 75,253 rows × 30 fields. Every line has the same number of fields, and all count values arenon-negative integers.
- The header starts with a UTF‑8 BOM, so the first column reads as \ufeffGene unless you read it with utf-8-sig.
- The file is two tables stuck together side by side. Column 27 is empty, and column 28 is a second gene-key column (gene) that belongs only to the last sample, 148-12-13-14_S10.
- The two gene-key columns don't line up. Gene and gene disagree on 74,691 of 75,253 rows (they drift apart from about row 83 onwards). gene also has 44 trailing blank rows.
  - Reading sample 148 against Gene assigns its counts to the wrong genes. For example, ACTB is 8,340 when keyed correctly but 0 when keyed naively; PTPRC is 1,136 versus 0.
  - Sample 148 has to be keyed by its own gene column.
- Sample 148 uses an older gene annotation. 171 symbols appear only in its key column and 215 only in the main one, and they are renames of the same genes (BAI1 → ADGRB1, AMICA1 → JAML, GPR56 → ADGRG1). That points to a different processing pipeline.
- What the gene keys contain: symbols only, with no Ensembl IDs or versions, so gene length and biotype can't be checked. 54,072 keys are NONCODE lncRNAIDs (NONHSAG…), 1,885 are MIR loci and 19,081 are other symbols. There are no MT- genes and no XIST. No Excel date damage was found and there are no duplicate keys. 75,038 keys are shared by all 27 samples.

Sample-to-GSM mapping: all 27 GSMs map one-to-one to all 27 count columns, with nothing left over. Getting there needed name normalisation: dropping a_bam suffix, and treating _ and - as the same (e.g. MGH51-081413 ↔ MGH51_081413, 070215-1 ↔ 070215_1_bam). The parsed annotation columns agree with the raw characteristics JSON. The patient ID column is labelled UNVERIFIED, but it matches the title and description for every sample.

2. Units, repeated samples, timing (actual outputs)

- 13 patients, 27 samples, two platforms: GPL18573 (NextSeq; batches 1–3) and GPL11154 (HiSeq 2000; batches 4–5). Response (R/NR) is recorded per patient, not per biopsy, and the response criterion and assessment time are not given.
- Repeated samples: IPIPD1001 has 7 samples, including 2 and 3 specimens from the same dates (day 434 and day 439). Within-patient correlation (r0.93–0.97) is no higher than the correlation between that patient's day −6 and day 153 samples, so these look like separate specimens or regions, not technical replicates. Patients 530 (3), 200, 253, 27, 51, 98 and PDL002 (2 each) also have repeats.
- Biopsy dates: reading the dates in the titles as MMDDYY gives one consistent treatment start date per patient for 24 of 26 dated samples. Two exceptions:
  - Patient 200 (MGH200_FFPE_031116, labelled PRE but at day +21): its two samples imply start dates 27 days apart. Either the sample was taken on treatment or the date is wrong.
  - Patient 98 is off by 1 day, which doesn't matter.
- Patient 27's PRE biopsy was taken 433 days before treatment started.
- Regimens are mixed: anti-PD1, anti-PDL1 and anti-PD1+anti-CTLA4. Prior therapy, line of therapy and biopsy site are not in the annotations.
- Sex markers: chrY genes (RPS4Y1, DDX3Y, KDM5D) are consistent within every patient with more than one sample, so there is no sign of mislabelledsamples. This is weak evidence, though: XIST is missing and melanomas often lose chrY.

4. Power limits (actual calculations)

- Rank tests can't reach significance at this size. The smallest possible exact two-sided Wilcoxon p-value is 0.029 for 4 vs 4 and 0.0079 for 5 vs 5. Either way, no gene could pass Bonferroni or BH correction across about 15,000 genes.
- Parametric tests are almost as limited. With a t-test at Bonferroni α ≈ 3.3e‑6, power is 5% for an effect size of d = 4 with 5 per group, and 0.8% with 4 per group. Even at α = 1e‑3, 80% power needs d ≈ 4.0 (5 per group) or d ≈ 5.2 (4 per group).

5. Reproducible analysis plan (proposed, not run)

1. Ingest: read with utf-8-sig, split the file into its two tables, key sample 148 by its own column, keep the 75,038 shared genes, and harmonise tocurrent HGNC symbols. Primary universe: protein-coding genes only (NONCODE and MIR as a sensitivity check).
   - Requires an HGNC/biotype lookup table, which I did not have.
2. Freeze the sample manifest before looking at results: the 8-patient core set, one biopsy per patient, with the inclusion rules written down.
3. Gene filtering and normalisation: filterByExpr-style filtering on the eligible samples only, TMM normalisation, then voom with sample-quality weights.DESeq2 as a cross-check.
4. Pre-registered hypotheses first: a small set of published scores (e.g. IFN‑γ/T-cell-inflamed, CD8A, PD‑L1) with their direction stated in advance andtested at patient level. Report effect sizes with CIs and exact permutation p-values (70 possible splits for 4 vs 4).
   - Genome-wide limma is descriptive only: report BH-adjusted values and expect nothing to pass.
5. Design checks:
   - Model ~ response versus ~ batch + response. With batch, only the batch 3/4 samples carry information (3 vs 2), so report the residual degrees offreedom and whether the design is fully determined.
   - No + regimen term: there are too few degrees of freedom.
   - Check the design matrix rank, PCA coloured by batch, platform, response and FFPE, plus library size, detected genes and NONCODE fraction against response.
6. Sensitivity checks:
   - Batch 3+4 only.
   - Drop batch 1 (FFPE).
   - Anti-PD1 monotherapy only.
   - Broad 10-patient set (adding 200 and 27).
   - Leave-one-patient-out.
   - Permutation of labels within batch.
   - Count-model versus rank-based results.
   - Protein-coding versus all keys.
7. Not to be done:
   - Pooling ON samples or repeat specimens as if they were independent patients (IPIPD1001 alone would make up 7 of 27 rows).
   - Fitting a classifier or reporting AUC from 8–10 patients.

6. Where to stop and what to claim

I would stop after step 4 and the design checks: report pre-specified scores as exploratory associations measured within batch, with the confoundingstated.

"Predictive biomarker" is not supportable here, because it needs:
- an untreated or non-ICB comparison arm to separate prediction from prognosis;
- a defined response endpoint;
- a single regimen or an adequately sized one per regimen;
- independent, pre-specified validation, e.g. in other public pretreatment melanoma ICB cohorts.

None of these exists in this dataset. At most a finding here is a candidate pretreatment correlate of response that needs external validation.
